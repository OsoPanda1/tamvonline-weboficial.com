## 1. Foundational principle

A system is not valid merely because someone approves it. Its validity must be assessed against the available evidence, the quality of its methods, its reproducibility, security, privacy, ethical consequences, traceability, governance, and the limits of the evaluation itself.

VETRA does not replace discipline-specific peer review, security audits, legal advice, privacy assessments, or accredited conformity assessment. It coordinates evidence and review activities into a transparent, versioned decision process.

## 2. The VETRA axiom

`KNOWLEDGE ≠ MEMORY ≠ BELIEF ≠ REASONING ≠ DECISION ≠ AUTHORITY ≠ EXECUTION ≠ EVIDENCE ≠ IMPACT ≠ LEARNING`

No subsystem may silently assume the properties or authority of another.

A language model can generate reasoning, hypotheses, summaries, and test ideas. Reasoning is not a decision; memory is not truth; a cited record is not proof of the underlying claim; a decision is not a legal authorization; and a score is not a certification.

## 3. Legitimacy model

`Legitimacy = Identity + Authority + Policy + Evidence + Execution + Traceability + Governance + Human Oversight`

This is a conceptual checklist, not a mathematically proven physical law. In operational use, each term must be mapped to observable controls and a named responsible party.

**Fail-closed rule:** if a predefined critical control fails, the workflow must stop, suspend, or require remediation regardless of any aggregate score. A missing data point is not automatically a failure, but it must be classified as unknown, documented, or not applicable with justification. Unknowns must not be silently scored as verified.

## 4. Governing principles

- Evidence before assertion.
- Independence before approval.
- Reproducibility before a claim of validation.
- Security before deployment.
- Privacy by design before scale.
- Ethics before unmitigated impact.
- Traceability before trust.
- Governance before delegated autonomy.
- Metaevaluation before certainty.
- Continuous monitoring and reassessment.
- Human accountability for consequential decisions.
- Proportionate scrutiny based on the artifact's maturity and risk.

## 5. What VETRA evaluates

VETRA evaluates whether a proposal's claims are proportionate to its evidence and whether the decision process itself is sufficiently independent, reproducible, transparent, and contestable.

The framework covers scientific validity, technical robustness, security, privacy, ethics, reproducibility, governance, social impact, economic viability, research integrity, cognitive reliability, metaevaluation, evidence provenance, and future consequences.

## 6. Human authority and AI assistance

AI components may help retrieve sources, normalize metadata, compare versions, flag contradictions, draft reviewer questions, and identify missing evidence. They must state uncertainty, preserve source links, and be checked by a qualified human reviewer.

AI must not:
- invent references, authors, test results, signatures, reviewer identities, or approvals;
- certify its own outputs or systems;
- silently override a reviewer or the published policy;
- turn the absence of a record into proof of nonexistence;
- make consequential legal, ethical, financial, or publication decisions without accountable human review.

## 7. Evidence and reproducibility

Every material conclusion should be connected to the evidence examined, the version of the object, the assessment date, the method used, known limitations, and the responsible reviewer. Reproducibility expectations must reflect the domain, risk, lawful access to data, and stage of development.

A cryptographic hash can detect a change when the exact content is compared. A hash alone does not prove authorship, truth, legal ownership, or the time at which the content existed. Signatures and trusted timestamps require a real implementation, key-management policy, and validation procedure.

## 8. Review outcomes

The output should be an evidence-based report with one of these statuses: received, incomplete, under independent review, minor revision, major revision, accepted subject to conditions, rejected with reasons, suspended, withdrawn, or revoked after a documented review.

Until an independent governance and assurance process exists, VETRA outputs must be described as **internal assessment reports**, not as internationally accredited certifications.

## 9. Appeal and continuous oversight

Applicants must be able to challenge factual errors, procedural failures, undisclosed conflicts, or newly available evidence. Appeals should be handled by reviewers who were not responsible for the initial decision whenever feasible. Material changes, newly discovered risks, and incidents trigger reassessment.

## 10. Concise technical statement

**VETRA Ω∞ is a proposed multidisciplinary evidence-assurance and metaevaluation framework that structures independent human review, risk-based technical checks, reproducibility analysis, governance controls, and traceable decisions. Its purpose is to determine whether a claim is sufficiently supported for a declared use—not to manufacture certainty or substitute for external authority.**
